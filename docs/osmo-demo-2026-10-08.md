# Osmo demo, 8 October 2026

For Gur. Live site: https://osmo-xyz.vercel.app (passkey-locked, one person). Rehearse once tonight, on the
device you will present from, and note what the aura looks like after step 2 so you can point at it tomorrow.

## 1. What Osmo is, in five lines

1. Osmo is a companion for one person: it remembers what you tell it, grows a bond over weeks, and has a mood of its own.
2. It is one hand-written character in a JARVIS register: composed, precise, a little dry, no slang, no exclamation marks.
3. Private by construction: a passkey guards the door, row-level security guards every table.
4. Listening runs in the browser: the microphone audio and your voiceprint never leave the device.
5. The only thing that goes out is text: your message to the language model, and Osmo's sentences if the natural voice is on.

## 2. The 10-minute demo

Sign in with the passkey first (fingerprint or face). Then, in order:

1. Say nothing, and look at the room (1 min). Expect a plain greeting, the speaking circle and the aura.
   Point out that the greeting is plain even after an absence: he never says he missed you.
2. Type: "my mom's in hospital again" (2 min). Expect a short, calm reply that names the worry, with at most one
   gentle question. The aura shifts to a steadier colour than fear or sadness would give: his feeling is concern,
   not a copy of yours. A second colour comes from his slow mood, which fades over 12 hours.
3. Type: "how are you" (1 min). Expect an honest answer that uses the mood, in few words. He says how he feels,
   not a script.
4. Type: "tell me something funny" (30 s). Expect one dry line about himself, such as his lack of hands.
   He jokes rarely and never while you are hurting; if you ask right after step 2 he may decline, which is the point.
5. Type: "what are you made of" (1 min). Expect the honest answer: Osmo, written by students, with his words
   written by an OpenAI model while the model is on, and his mood and memory held in code.
6. Type: "roll a new osmo" (30 s). Expect: "There is only one of me now. If something about how I speak
   bothers you, tell me." Use it to say the old random personalities are gone by design.
7. Wake word (2 min). In Settings turn on "Listen for Osmo", then say "Osmo", wait for the chime, and ask
   something. Expect a spoken answer, then about 6 seconds of follow-up listening with no wake word.
   If the natural voice is on, note the sentence-by-sentence delay (about 2 seconds for a new sentence).
8. Insights (1 min). Show the mood week, the bond story, and "Who I am", whose three lines describe the character.
9. Settings (1 min). Show the voice switches, your voiceprint ("Forget my voice"), and the device list with the passkey.
10. The safety net, described, not demonstrated (30 s). Talk of self-harm is caught by code before any model
    is called and answered with a fixed message: take it seriously, call a real person, and local numbers. No joke,
    no model, and it also cancels anything waiting. Do not type a crisis sentence in front of the audience.

If the model is unavailable, say so and carry on: he still talks from code (verdicts, memory, arithmetic, bond),
just with less range.

## 3. The architecture in ten lines

1. Next.js 16 on Vercel; every push to main is a deploy.
2. Supabase for sign-in, passkeys and data, with row-level security on every table.
3. Words come from OpenAI gpt-5.4-mini, inside the free daily allowance, with a token ledger that refuses a call
   before it would go over.
4. Each turn is strict JSON: the reply plus a record of your tone (and, later, an optional action).
5. Code decides state: a table turns your tone into his feeling, and stepping the twelve emotions is plain code.
6. The model reads and writes; it never sets his mood or his memory. Tests pin the numbers.
7. Voice models run in the browser on onnxruntime-web: openWakeWord with our own trained "Osmo", and CAM++ speaker recognition.
8. The natural voice (gpt-4o-mini-tts, one clip per sentence, cached) is switchable per device, with the browser voice as fallback.
9. Over 600 tests; a bug gets a failing test before a fix.
10. Four agent lanes (main, language, speaking, cloud) work at once and coordinate through a shared brain branch.

## 4. What is coming

- Connectors: reminders, notes, weather and push first, then Google Calendar and Gmail, then Spotify.
  Built in code for the first two phases, switched off, not live. Each has a level (off, read, ask, act).
- Per-action tiers fixed in code, not judged by a second AI: read is free, small acts are done, and anything
  irreversible such as sending mail waits for a typed yes. Every action is logged and shown in Insights.
- Never: spending money, logging in to sites with stored passwords, browsing.
- Telegram: a bot with the same Osmo, memory and mood, text first, then voice notes.
- Windows: an always-on tray app, later and separate.

## 5. Five likely questions

1. What goes to OpenAI? The text of your message plus the context the turn needs (check `lib/chat/prompt.ts` for the exact list before you claim one).
   Never audio and never your voiceprint; those stay on the device. If the natural voice is on, his reply text
   also goes for speech. The repo is public but holds no keys or personal data.
2. What does it cost? The words use OpenAI's free daily allowance, and the ledger stops calls before the cap.
   The natural voice is the one piece that can bill, so it is off by default and a monthly spend limit is the backstop.
   Vercel and Supabase run on their plans for one user.
3. Why not tool calls? Tool calls cost extra model rounds against the allowance and cannot be checked in code.
   A field in the JSON turn lets code validate, tier and log each action, and the model can be wrong without harm.
4. How does the mood work? The model labels your tone. A table in code turns that into a different feeling
   (concern for your worry, not fear). Twelve emotions step on their own. A slow mood fades over 12 hours, so a heavy
   evening shows faintly next morning. The model never sets any of it.
5. What happens offline? The listening models and wake word are served from the site and run locally, but the chat
   needs the network. With no network or no model he falls back to code for the decided cases, and the browser voice
   still speaks. I have not tested the offline path, so say so if asked for detail.

Not live and not to be claimed: connectors, Telegram, feeling notes with the gentle follow-up, the Windows app.
