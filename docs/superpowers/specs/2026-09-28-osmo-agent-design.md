# Osmo as an agent: a language model for his words, his own heart underneath

Date: 2026-09-28. Revised 2026-09-29 for OpenAI and the free daily allowance, then reviewed from six angles and corrected the same day. Status: design; the language lane builds it, the cloud lane keeps this document and reviews the code.

**Superseded in part on 2026-09-29, evening.** Gur made his design choices with the language lane (`decisions.md` on `brain`, "the AI conversation, Gur's design choices so far"), and language's own spec, `2026-09-29-osmo-ai-conversation-design.md`, is the one being built. Where the two differ, that one wins. The differences: the first build is the conversation only, with memory tools, searching the past, web search and summaries later; the turn stays in the browser (mood, bond, memory saving and the database writes) and the route only writes the words, so items 3 and 7 under "The shape of the change", "What stays deterministic", "His tools", the phases and the contract below describe a shape that is not being built; code keeps the crisis reply and every reply that saves or changes something (name, facts, taught words, re-roll, verdicts), and the model writes the rest; guests never reach the model; each request carries his memory facts, the last 20 messages with crisis messages and guest lines left out, and his mood, bond and personality; Osmo's share is 700,000 and 70,000 tokens a day with a 10% margin, so the route stops at 630,000 and 63,000; the route's logic lives in `lib/chat/**`, not `lib/agent/`. What still stands, and what the cloud lane keeps current: "The model and the API" (the allowance and its four rules, the listed models, the request fields, the counting, the errors and the fallback), "The first-day check", the personality piece, and the risks. The Asks this spec made of main for a server-side turn are withdrawn on `desks/cloud.md`.

**Language's spec, `56d2784` on `main` (2026-09-30), settles the rest of phase 1**, and these parts of this document go with it: the conversation has its own OpenAI project and key, `OSMO_CHAT_OPENAI_KEY`, so "the key is shared" below is no longer true and `/api/speak` keeps `OPENAI_API_KEY` on a project without sharing; an explicit `OSMO_CHAT=on` switch on top of the cap, and no fallback to the other key; the ledger is `ai_calls`, a reservation and a signed settling row per call, insert-only, in place of `token_ledger` and `add_tokens`; only dated snapshots on the list (`gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17`, with `gpt-4.1-mini-2025-04-14` as the alternative), and `response.model` checked after every call; plain `fetch` to the Responses endpoint rather than the `openai` package, 10 seconds and no retry; `max_output_tokens` 300; a ceiling of 20,000 estimated tokens per call, the estimate in UTF-8 bytes plus a per-item overhead; the history is the last 20 messages with crisis lines removed; the fallback reasons are `off`, `allowance`, `error`, `empty` and `crisis`, and a 401 never locks the room; the files are `app/api/chat/route.ts`, `lib/chat/**` and `scripts/chat-probe.mts`. Phases 2 to 4 below stay the plan, to be re-fitted onto what phase 1 builds once it has shipped.

## Goal
Osmo answers almost any question and talks like a person, while staying the same Osmo: his mood, values, personality donors, bond and memories keep shaping what he says. An OpenAI model writes his words, inside the free daily allowance Gur's account gets for traffic shared with OpenAI. His heart, brain and bond stay rule-based and run on every turn, because they are what make him consistent, learnable and his. Everything he knows about Gur lives in Supabase and he reads and writes it himself through tools, so he becomes an agent that runs off the database rather than a page that pattern-matches sentences.

## Non-goals
- Changing the voice (spec `2026-09-27-osmo-voice-design.md`, shipped). He speaks, wakes to his name and tells Gur from a guest. This plan fits inside it: the route is called from the same `sendText` path, and a guest gets exactly what the voice spec allows.
- More than one user.
- Replacing the heart, brain, bond or genome with the model's own guesses about how Osmo feels. They stay as code.
- Letting the model change its own state directly. It sees his state and can ask for memories to be saved; the code decides what his mood and bond do.
- Paying for conversation. Every model call stays inside the allowance; when the day's share is used up he answers from code until the counter resets.

## Where Osmo stands today
Read for this plan: every file under `app/`, `components/`, `lib/agent/`, `lib/shell/`, all six specs, the shared brain (`lanes.md`, `project.md`, `decisions.md` and the four desks on the `brain` branch), the live Supabase schema (project `agent-memory`) and the Vercel project `osmo`. Checked against `main` at `8b50b4f`, which carries the voice. Tests: 612 pass there (690 on local `main` with the natural voice, per the speaking desk). Lint: one warning. Typecheck passes after `next typegen`.

What works well and should be kept:
- The heart (`heart.ts`, `cues.ts`, `events.ts`), the brain (`brain.ts`, `dilemmas.ts`), the bond (`bond/`), the genome (`personality/`) and the mood theme. All pure, all tested, all cheap to run every turn.
- The crisis check (`safety.ts`). It must stay deterministic and decide the reply before anything else does; today it runs right after the heart step and before the bond, and that order is kept.
- Persistence (`agent-state.ts`, `load.ts`), the lock, the panels, and the room itself.
- The voice (`lib/voice/`), and with it two things the route inherits: one message path, `sendText(text, { via, speaker, greet })`, for typed and spoken words alike; and the guest rules in `lib/voice/guest.ts`, where a voice that is not Gur's sees nothing of his and changes nothing.

What holds him back:
- Every reply is a template chosen by a regular expression. `talk.ts` recognizes about 25 intents; anything else falls through to "I'm not sure I follow" or "Could you explain it? I'll remember." That is why he cannot answer most questions.
- `app/assistant.tsx` carries a 200-line chain of special cases (names from chat history, pending explanations, hand-rolled arithmetic, a 20-entry built-in fact list) that each fix one conversation and break another. The code review below lists several of those breaks.
- The personality donors can only flavor a template. The first personality spec already noted this ceiling: "the voice will read as a flavored version of the same replies, not 100 truly different speakers." A model can actually sound like The Night-Shift Nurse.
- He remembers facts (`memory_facts`) but not conversations. "How did the exam go?" is impossible today.

## The shape of the change

```
browser (room)  --->  POST /api/chat (Vercel, holds the key)  --->  OpenAI (gpt-5.4-mini)
      ^                     |            ^
      |                     v            |  tools: remember, forget, search the past,
      |               Supabase (as Gur, RLS)      define a word, a story, a dilemma
      |               token_ledger: today's tokens per pool, checked before every call
      +---- the reply, the new state, today's use ----+
```

1. **A server route holds the key.** Today the whole app runs in the browser with the publishable Supabase key. A model API key cannot live there. A Next.js route handler at `app/api/chat/route.ts` runs on Vercel with `OPENAI_API_KEY` as a server-only environment variable, or `CHATGPT_KEY`, the alias `project.md` records and the name Gur typed into `.env.local`; the route reads `OPENAI_API_KEY` first, exactly as speaking's `/api/speak` does. It is the same key the speaking voice uses. Where it stands: the key is in `.env.local` only; the Vercel project has neither name yet, and only Gur adds it. `/api/speak` and `lib/server/auth.ts` are speaking's, in local commit `3902078` on `main`, not pushed as of 2026-09-29, so neither is in `8b50b4f`; `lib/server/auth.ts` exports `bearerToken(request)` and `requireUser(request, lookup?)`, which returns the user or null, with `lookup` injectable so a route tests without Supabase (speaking's desk). The key is never `NEXT_PUBLIC_`.
2. **The route acts as Gur, not as an admin, and only as Gur.** The browser sends its Supabase access token in the `Authorization` header. `requireUser` verifies it; the route then builds a Supabase client that carries that same bearer token, so every read and write still goes through the same row-level security as today. No service-role key anywhere. A request without a valid token gets 401. A valid token is any token the Supabase project issues, and the ledger below is per user, so a token alone is not enough: the route compares the user id `requireUser` returns with `OSMO_OWNER_ID`, a server setting holding Gur's Supabase user id (a uuid, not a secret), and any other user gets 403 before anything is loaded, saved or called. Without that line every account the project ever issues a token to would get its own daily share from the one key, and sign-ups are still open (`project.md`, Supabase auth). Closing them (`docs/osmo-deploy.md`, step 1) is a precondition of phase 1 going live; the 403 is the guard for when that slips. With `OSMO_OWNER_ID` unset the route never calls the model and answers from code with `reason: "no_owner"`.
3. **The turn moves to the server.** The route loads his state and memory, runs the deterministic steps (heart step, then crisis, then bond), builds a system prompt from the result, calls the model with his tools, runs the tools against Supabase, saves everything, and returns the reply. The browser becomes what it should be: a room that shows the words and the mood.
4. **He stays inside the allowance.** Gur's account gets a free daily token budget for traffic shared with OpenAI, split into a mini-model pool and a large-model pool, and his investing agents use most of it. Osmo's share (`decisions.md`, 2026-09-29, raised the same evening from 500,000 and 50,000) is 700,000 tokens a day from the mini pool and 70,000 from the large pool, with a 10% margin, so he stops at 630,000 and 63,000. OpenAI does not stop at the limit; it bills what goes over. So the route keeps its own ledger in Supabase, counts every token of every call (input, output and reasoning), refuses a call that could cross the day's cap, and lets the code answer instead. The caps are server settings Gur can change without a code change, and they are also the switch: with no cap set, the route never calls the model. The section "The model and the API" has the exact rules.
5. **The old chain becomes the fallback.** When the model is unreachable (no key, rate limit, outage, timeout) or the day's share is used up, the route answers with today's `processTurn` and template replies. Osmo gets duller, never silent. `talk.ts` stays for that reason and stops growing.
6. **`sendText` stays the one door.** Typed and spoken messages already arrive through it, and every reply already leaves through `deliver`, which hands the text to the voice. The route call goes inside `sendText`, so the voice engine, the guest marking and the "Someone else" label keep working unchanged.
7. **A guest gets a guest.** The request carries `speaker` and the voice engine's `greet` flag. For a guest the route builds the prompt from the guest view (no name, no memory, no history, no bond, no cause), tells the model it is speaking with someone who is not its owner, offers only `define`, and that in its guest form (no taught words, no cache write, as the room does today): `search_past` would read his messages and mood days, `remember`, `forget`, `experience_story`, `pose_dilemma` and `note_shared` change what is his, and `web_search` is billed, so none of them is in a guest request. It discards the turn's state and effects, greets a guest's first line when the request says so (`greet`, applied with `greetGuest` from `lib/voice/guest.ts` before saving, never on a crisis reply, on the fallback reply too, so the saved line is the spoken line), and saves both lines with `speaker = 'guest'`. That is the voice spec's Part 2, applied to the model. A guest's tokens come out of the same ledger, because the route is acting as Gur either way.

## What the model is told, every turn
The system prompt (the `instructions` of the request) is assembled from his state by a pure function, `lib/agent/prompt.ts`, so it can be tested without the API. In order, stable content first so it caches:

1. **Who he is.** Osmo, one person's companion, built by students, professional and composed like JARVIS, with dry wit. Everything he says is plain speakable text: no markdown, lists, emoji, brackets or symbols, because a voice reads it aloud. Short replies by default, one question at a time. He has opinions and defends them, gently. He never talks down.
2. **The rules that stay code.** He is told which things are handled for him and not to improvise them: the crisis reply, the guest greeting, re-rolling, saving memories (use the tool, do not claim to remember without it). This block never changes, so it sits with the first.
3. **His personality donors.** The six donor names and their organs, written as guidance rather than data: "Your voice comes from The Victorian Butler: formal, full sentences, no contractions. Your humor comes from The Grumpy Professor: dry, rare." Every organ's written material goes in: the voice openers and elaboration, the humor lines whatever their style, the slang tags, the catchphrases. Today all of that is stored on all 100 donors and used by nothing (see "Personality piece 2, absorbed"). The model is told to use it sparingly and in the professional register, which is a judgement a template could not make. This is where the donors finally sound different.
4. **His values.** The five moral weights in words ("you weigh honesty most, then kindness"), and his outlook ("you lean toward hope, because of what you have experienced").
5. **His memory of Gur.** Every `memory_facts` row as a sentence, the same sentences the Memory panel shows. Words Gur taught him. Gur's own frequent words, so the model knows "valo" is not a typo.
6. **The relationship.** The bond stage as it stood before this turn, how many days they have talked, milestones reached, any milestone due to be mentioned, and how long Gur has been away. Written as instructions per stage, matching the bond spec's table: a stranger gets formal precision, an old friend gets easy familiarity.
7. **How he feels right now, and why.** `feelingPhrase`, the dominant emotions, `session.cause`, today's mood so far from `mood_days`, what happened last time they talked, and the `told` lines from the deterministic steps ("Gur just shared sad news; your sadness rose"). This block changes every turn, so it goes last.

The conversation itself is the last `historyWindow(used, cap)` rows of `messages` (30, or 10 once the mini pool is past 80% of its cap for the day), both roles, with `speaker` null, as `ownerHistory` filters today, in `created_at, id` order. Each row is sent as a plain text item, `{ role: "user" | "assistant", content: text }` (`agent` maps to `assistant`), and the new message follows them as the last `user` item; it is not in the table yet, because the route saves both rows after the reply. A guest's input is the new message alone. The model's reasoning is never stored or replayed across turns: every turn is rebuilt from `messages`, the request does not chain on a previous response, and OpenAI is asked not to store responses. The whole prompt should stay near 4,000 tokens, because every token comes out of the day's share.

## What stays deterministic, in order
Before the model sees anything, the route runs what `mind.ts` does today, in today's order: the heart step, then the crisis check, then the bond. The crisis check is the first step that chooses a reply, and a crisis message still moves his heart, as it does now. The main lane exports these steps from `mind.ts` as one function (answered 2026-09-29; main's answer named crisis, the heart step, bond, verdict and re-roll, and steps 5 and the exact shape below are asked of main on the cloud desk):

`prepareTurn(state, session, text, ctx): TurnResult & { told: string[]; signals: { feeling: boolean; event: boolean } }`, with the same `ctx: TurnContext` and `TurnResult` (`state`, `session`, `reply`, `effects`) as `processTurn`. It runs every branch `takeTurn` runs today except step 6, everyday conversation, with every guest gate inside it, and like `processTurn` returns `effects: []` for a guest. `told` is short English lines for the prompt ("Gur just shared sad news; your sadness rose", "Gur agreed with your last verdict", "Gur tried to argue you toward hope"). `signals` is what the bond step used, so a phase 2 tool can add what it missed without counting twice. When `reply` is non-null the route saves it and returns it, and the model is not called: in phase 1 that is the crisis reply, a verdict, the re-roll offer and its confirmation and nudges, made-of, closeness, met, a story, a dilemma, and every guest refusal. From phase 2 the story and dilemma branches become tools, and main turns the verdict and re-roll outcomes into `told` lines with `reply: null` so the model voices them; that is a second Ask, when phase 2 starts.

1. **Heart.** Gap and loneliness, cues, reactivity, one coupling and decay step, exactly as the heart block at the top of `takeTurn` does today, on every message including a crisis one. The new activations are what the prompt describes.
2. **Crisis.** `isCrisis` on the raw text. If it matches, the reply is `CRISIS_REPLY`; the heart-updated state is kept, `session.cause` becomes "you told me you're hurting" and any pending question is cleared, as `mind.ts` does; the bond is not recorded; the turn is saved and returned. The model is not called and no tokens are spent.
3. **Bond.** `recordTurn` with today's signals, computed once (finding 5): `understand` for a feeling, `classifyUserEvents` for an event, `ctx.userName` for the name, and the request's `day` (below) as its day. That is all of phase 1, which has no tools. From phase 2 the model can report a feeling or event the regexes missed through `note_shared`; `recordTurn` has already counted the message by then and cannot be called again without counting a second message, so `bond.ts` gets a second function, `noteShared(bond, kind, now)`, that adds to `shared` and reaches `firstFeeling` or `firstEvent` without touching `messages` or `days`, and the route calls it only when `signals` says that kind was not already counted. A milestone reached this way is mentioned on the next turn, which `toMention` already allows. Both functions are main's.
4. **Pending answers.** A yes or no while a dilemma verdict is pending, and "yes, roll" while a re-roll is pending, are still handled by code, with the code's reply in phase 1 (above).
5. **Life events and arguments.** `classifyUserEvents` and `detectArgument` still run so `applyEvent` and `argueOutlook` keep learning. These branches apply their change to his state, add a `told` line, and return `reply: null`, because the model writes the acknowledgement itself. Today `takeTurn` answers those turns; this is the one place `prepareTurn` differs from it.

**The session travels with the request.** `Session` (`pending`, `last`, `dilemmasSeen`, `cause`, `turns`, `awaitingReroll`, from `mind.ts`) lives only in the browser's React state today; no table holds it and `persistTurn` never touches it. It stays the browser's: the body carries it, the route returns it as it stands after `prepareTurn` (or `processTurn` on fallback) and any tool effects (in phase 2 `pose_dilemma` sets `pending` and `dilemmasSeen`, `experience_story` sets `cause`), and the browser keeps what comes back in place of today's `setSession(turn.session)`. A guest turn returns the session it was sent, unchanged, as the room leaves it untouched today. The route validates its shape and treats a bad or missing one as `newSession()`. Nothing is trusted that is not trusted today: the browser already writes `agent_state` directly.

**The day travels with the request too.** `saveMoodDay` keys `mood_days` by `localDay(Date.now())` and `recordTurn` counts a talked day by `localDay(s.now)`; in the browser that is Gur's day, on Vercel it would be UTC, so late-evening turns would land on the next day and the Insights panel would disagree with the bond. The body carries `day`, the browser's `localDay(Date.now())`; the route accepts it only if it matches `YYYY-MM-DD` and is within one day of the server's UTC date, else it uses the UTC date. `recordTurn` takes it as an optional `day` on `TurnSignals` (defaulting to `localDay(now)` for the browser fallback and tests), passed through `prepareTurn`'s context so the fallback `processTurn` uses the same day; `saveMoodDay` takes it instead of calling `localDay` on the server; the prompt's "today's mood so far" reads `mood_days` with the same `day`. The ledger's `dayKey` ignores it and stays UTC, because that is OpenAI's reset. Main's change, on the cloud desk.

After the model replies, the route saves `agent_state`, `mood_days` and any effects from tools through `persistTurn`, and inserts the two `messages` rows itself, as `saveMessages` in `assistant.tsx` does today, so a guest turn can save its two lines while discarding state and effects. Today `loadState` and `persistTurn` (`lib/agent/agent-state.ts`, main's) use the browser's singleton client and `ensureSession()`, so they cannot run in the route unchanged; the Ask to main, next to `prepareTurn`, is a client parameter and the user id to write: `loadState(client)`, `persistTurn(client, userId, state, effects)`; the route passes its per-request client and the verified user, the browser callers pass the singleton and the session's user, so both paths share one save. The ledger rows are written by `ledger.ts` as each model call completes, not here.

## His tools
All run in the route, all through the per-request Supabase client, all with their inputs validated before they run. Each is small and has its own tests with a fake database. Every tool round trip is another model call, and its tokens count.

| Tool | What it does | Table |
|---|---|---|
| `remember(key, value)` | Saves a fact about Gur. Replaces the regexes in `lib/facts.ts` and the "Could you explain it? I'll remember" flow. Keys keep today's shapes (`name`, `likes`, `slang:bet`, `meaning:zorp`) so the Memory panel keeps working. | `memory_facts` |
| `forget(key)` | Removes a fact when Gur asks him to. | `memory_facts` |
| `search_past(query, days \| null)` | Full-text search over their old messages, returned with dates. This is what makes "what did I say about my exam" and "how was I doing last week" answerable. | `messages`, `mood_days` |
| `define(term)` | The existing `lookupWord` (taught words, built-in slang, cache, Datamuse, Wiktionary). Kept because it is cached, private and already handles offensive senses. On a guest turn it runs without taught words and without the cache write (voice spec, Part 2). | `word_lookups` |
| `experience_story()` | The existing `pickEvent` and `applyEvent`. Returns the story and his new feeling; the model tells it. | `event_log`, `emotion_associations` |
| `pose_dilemma(topic \| null)` | The existing `nextDilemma` or `findDilemma`, scored by `decide`. Returns the scenario, his choice and the values that drove it; the model explains and asks "Do you agree?" | `dilemma_log` |
| `note_shared(kind)` | The model reports that Gur shared a feeling or a life event; the route calls `noteShared` on the turn's bond when the regexes had not counted it. Replaces the feeling regexes for bond purposes from phase 2. | (bond in `agent_state`) |
| `web_search` | OpenAI's built-in web search, for current facts (the model's knowledge stops at August 2025). Off by default: a turn that uses it is treated as billed, because the search fee is billed and the turn's own tokens may be too (to confirm; see below). | (none) |

Web search changes a promise he makes today ("internet access is used only to look up word definitions"), and it is the one thing in this plan that costs money: a search is billed per call, and the turn's own tokens may be billed too (to confirm), so the plan treats a search turn as billed. It becomes a switch in Settings, off by default, stored in a new `settings` column on `agent_state`, with a daily number of searches next to it. When it is off the tool is not offered. His self-description is updated either way.

`define` moves the dictionary lookup to the server. `lookupWord` runs in the route with the server's `fetch`, so Datamuse and Wiktionary are called from Vercel rather than from Gur's browser, and `sendText` no longer runs `parseLookup` or `lookupWord` (both are on the phase 2 delete list). Only the term is sent to either site, as before. This supersedes the 2026-09-26 decision that the lookups run in the browser; language appends the new entry to `decisions.md` and retires the `connect-src` note on its desk when phase 2 ships. The fallback `processTurn` has no lookup step, so on a fallback turn a word question gets the rule-based reply.

## What he can newly do
- Answer general questions, explain things, help think through a problem, do arithmetic, and hold a real conversation about anything. The model does this on its own.
- Answer about the world as it is now, through web search, if Gur switches it on.
- Remember the thread of their life together, not only facts: "You mentioned the exam last Tuesday. How did it go?" comes from `search_past` in phase 2 and from episodes in phase 3.
- Sound like his donors. A Victorian Butler Osmo and a Skater Osmo will read differently for the first time.
- Have an opinion in his own voice about a dilemma, a story or a piece of news, weighted by his values and mood.

## Personality piece 2, absorbed
The personality spec promised a "piece 2": about 25 topics with follow-ups, short-term memory, and a larger slang dictionary. It was never written. Where it stands:

- **The slang dictionary shipped** with the dictionary spec. Done.
- **Topics with follow-ups were never built.** The nearest things are the 20 one-line facts in `agentKnowledge` (matched by substring, which the review found buggy) and the "explain it and I'll remember" flow. Twenty-five hand-written topics would be twenty-five more regexes and templates, and the replayed chat log in `chatlog.test.ts` shows real messages ("do it jiggle when you walk") do not fit templates. The model makes topics free and unlimited (phase 1), and web search covers the present (phase 3). Follow-ups become `search_past` (phase 2) and episodes with a follow-up date (phase 3).
- **Short-term memory exists in fragments**, each solving one incident: `session.cause` (why he feels this way), the pending dilemma, the last question he asked (so "Gur" is read as a name), the words of the last six messages (for typos only), and `recallReply`, which quotes the last two lines back verbatim. Nothing tracks what was talked about. In the route, the conversation window in the prompt gives all of this for free; `cause` and the pending dilemma are kept as named lines in the prompt because they are state the model cannot see otherwise.

One more thing piece 2 would have run into. The material meant to give each personality "more to say" is already written and already dead:

- Only 12 of the 100 donors may supply voice, humor, slang or quirks (`personality/modern.ts`, not recorded in any spec).
- Of those 12, only two have dry humor, and `flavor.ts` speaks no other style. So ten of the twelve have no humor at all.
- `voice.openers`, `voice.elaboration`, `slang.says` and `quirks.phrases` are stored for every donor and read by no code path since the professional-voice change.

So today the voice organ changes nothing in how he sounds: `flavor.ts` drops a trailing question only below verbosity 0.3 and expands contractions only above formality 0.75, and none of the twelve present-day donors is in either range, so the only trace of a voice donor is his name in the made-of readout. A donor shows in a reply only as a rare framed slang joke from a hand-picked list of seven donors in `bond/lines.ts` and, for the two dry donors, one of their three dry lines from friend stage on. Piece 2 as designed would have poured more templates into that same narrow pipe. In this plan the donor material becomes prompt guidance instead, which is the only way a teasing or punning donor can be funny without being tone-deaf: the model reads the mood and the stage before it decides whether a line fits. The present-day restriction can then be reconsidered as a register rule ("sound like now") rather than a roster cut.

## Phases

### Phase 0. Fix what the review found
Small, mechanical, keeps every test green. Ships on its own first so the later phases start clean. The findings are listed at the end.

### Phase 1. The route, the model and the ledger, no tools
- `app/api/chat/route.ts`: verify the token and the owner, load state and memory, run `prepareTurn`, read today's use, build the prompt, call the model through the ledger, save, return.
- `lib/agent/prompt.ts` (pure) and its tests.
- `lib/agent/allowance.ts` (pure) and its tests: the listed models, which pool a model belongs to, the estimate for a call, and the decision "may this call be made today". The ledger is not optional and not phase 2: OpenAI bills what goes over the allowance, so the counter goes live with the first model call.
- The `token_ledger` table (main applies the migration; the SQL is in "Storage changes").
- **Every message goes through the route, guest turns included.** `sendText` no longer calls `processTurn`, `persistTurn` or `saveMessages`: the route runs `prepareTurn` and saves `agent_state`, the two `messages` rows and `mood_days` for every turn, a fallback turn as much as a model turn, so the heart steps exactly once and the rows are written exactly once. When the fallback's `processTurn` has no reply, the route answers with `fallbackReply` from `talk.ts`, which is where today's browser chain ends. The rest of the chain in `sendText` (the pending explanation, the name flows, recall, taught slang and plain facts, arithmetic, dictionary lookups, unknown topics, `answerFromMemory` and `agentKnowledge`) is not run in phase 1: it stays in the file, unreachable, until phase 2 deletes it. So in phase 1 taught slang, plain facts and word lookups are not saved or looked up by code; the model answers them from the prompt and its own knowledge until `remember` and `define` arrive in phase 2. `learnFromMessage` (Gur's vocabulary) keeps running in the browser as today.
- The browser sends `{ text, speaker, session, day, greet }` with the token from inside `sendText`, waits for the finished reply, and hands `deliver` one string, because the voice speaks a reply as one utterance and the typewriter already follows its word timing. `sendText` appends Gur's line to the room at once and sets `thinking`, which shows today's "One moment…" line, makes `sendText` return `false` and locks the composer until the turn ends. The route call is always caught, because `sendText` never throws (`project.md`), and `thinking` is cleared in every outcome, so the room can never stay locked. If the fetch itself throws (offline) or the route answers anything but 200: a 401 means the session is gone, so the room calls `lockOsmo()` (the local sign-out in `lib/shell/passkeys.ts`; the existing `SIGNED_OUT` listener then sends the tab to `/lock`); anything else hands `deliver` the fixed line "I can't reach my words right now. Say that again in a moment." so the voice speaks it, and logs the status or error to the console. Nothing is saved on that path, because the route is now the only writer of `messages`: the two lines vanish on reload and the next attempt is a clean turn.
- The route does not stream in phase 1: a whole response is simpler to count and to fall back from, and the browser could not use the early words anyway. Streaming sentence by sentence is a later refinement.
- The mood theme updates from the state the route returns with the reply, and the response also carries today's use so the room can show it.
- Fallback to `processTurn` when the model call fails, the ledger says no, the caps are unset, or the key or owner is missing. The route then calls `processTurn(state, session, text, ctx)` with the state and session it had before `prepareTurn`, not the ones `prepareTurn` returned: `processTurn` is unchanged and runs the heart step, `recordTurn` and `applyEvent` itself, so this keeps each of them to exactly once per turn. The prepared result is dropped, and the `processTurn` result is what `persistTurn` saves and what the response returns as `state` and `session`.
- Vercel: the key is not there yet. Gur has it locally in `.env.local` as `CHATGPT_KEY` (`project.md`, Keys); before phase 1 goes live he adds `OPENAI_API_KEY` (or `CHATGPT_KEY`) to Vercel Production, and the same entry serves `/api/speak` once `naturalVoice` is switched on. The two cap variables and `OSMO_OWNER_ID` are new, and the caps are the switch (see Coordination). Supabase: the ledger.

After phase 1 he answers most questions. He does not yet remember anything new by himself.

### Phase 2. Tools, and the old chain retired
- The tools above, except `web_search`. Each with its own tests. The tool loop replays the model's output items in order (the SDK's `toResponseInputItems()`), appends the tool results, and counts every round into the ledger. At most four rounds a turn (`MAX_ROUNDS` in `openai.ts`; a round is one model call). If the fourth round still ends in a tool call, the loop stops: the text so far is used when it ends a sentence, else the fallback answers. A turn also carries a deadline of 40 seconds, taken when `ask` starts; each round's request is sent with the per-request `timeout` option set to the smaller of 15 seconds and the time left, so the client's 15-second setting is the per-round ceiling and the deadline is the per-turn one. A round that times out is counted at its estimate, as the counting rules say.

Delete `lib/facts.ts` regexes once `remember` covers them. `context.ts` keeps `turnView` and shrinks otherwise to what the fallback needs.

### Phase 3. Web search, and episodes
- `web_search` with the Settings switch, off by default because it is billed, and a cap on searches a day, counted from the `web_search_call` items in the responses into the ledger's `searches` column (this SDK has no per-request cap for built-in tools). When it is first switched on, the usage dashboard grouped by service tier shows whether a search turn's own tokens still land in the free tier; that settles the "to confirm" above.
- **Episodes.** Every 20 turns, or at the first message after a gap of an hour or more (measured from `lastAt`, the same gap the heart already uses), the route asks the model for a three-sentence first-person summary of the stretch that just ended ("Gur told me about his exam on Thursday. He was nervous. I said I would ask how it went.") with an optional follow-up date. The summary runs after the reply has been sent, in Next's `after()` from `next/server`, so the browser never waits for it; it still runs inside the same invocation and the same 300-second limit, which is why its timeout is short (see "Errors, timeouts and the fallback"). If it fails or the large pool is spent, it is tried again at the next trigger, not at once. Saved in a new `episodes` table. The most recent and the most relevant episodes (full-text match on the current message) go into the prompt. A due follow-up is mentioned when he greets Gur. This is what turns him from a chatbot with a fact list into someone who was there last week. Summaries are the one job for the large-model pool: a few calls a day where judgement matters more than speed.
- Full text search first. Vector search (`pgvector`) is a later upgrade if plain search proves too blunt; it needs an embedding provider and is not worth a second vendor yet.

### Phase 4. Being more human, once the plumbing is quiet
- **He starts conversations.** The welcome after a gap is generated from the last episode and any due follow-up instead of a fixed line per stage.
- **He notices patterns.** Once a week, a summary of `mood_days` and episodes lets him say "You have seemed tired all week" if it is true. Also on the large pool, in `after()`.
- **Evaluation.** A set of about 40 saved conversations graded for: speakable text, staying in character and stage, using memory correctly, and never inventing a memory. Run before any prompt change. Without this, prompt edits are guesswork.
- **Panels.** Insights shows episodes as part of "Our story" and shows today's and this week's tokens per pool against the caps, read from `token_ledger` in the browser under the same row-level security. Memory shows episodes under a fourth heading, editable and forgettable like facts.

## The model and the API, so nothing stale gets built
These are the current shapes as of this revision. Field names and types come from the `openai` Node SDK 7.23.0 type definitions (published 2026-09-23), which the cloud session read directly. The two pools, their model lists, standard-rate billing beyond them and the fact that OpenAI does not stop at the limit come from Gur's dashboard as recorded in `decisions.md` (2026-09-29). The per-request billing rule, the single input-plus-output count, the exclusions, the reset hour and the enrolment conditions come from OpenAI's help article on the programme (article 10306912), which the cloud session could read only through search-result snippets. Prices and per-model limits come from mirrors of OpenAI's pages and from other people's implementations, because the cloud session cannot reach OpenAI's own sites. All of those second-hand points are "to confirm", and the language lane checks them against the dashboard on the first live day.

### The allowance, and the rules it forces
- Two pools, from the dashboard (2026-09-29): up to 250,000 tokens a day across `gpt-5.4`, `gpt-5.2`, `gpt-5.1`, `gpt-5`, `gpt-4.1`, `gpt-4o`, `o1` and `o3`; up to 2.5 million tokens a day across `gpt-5.4-mini`, `gpt-5.4-nano`, `gpt-5-mini`, `gpt-5-nano`, `gpt-4.1-mini`, `gpt-4.1-nano`, `gpt-4o-mini`, `o3-mini` and `o4-mini`. Any other model, and anything over a limit, is billed at standard rates. OpenAI does not stop at the limit.
- From the help article (to confirm): "tokens" is one number, input plus output; the rule is per request, a request that would carry the day's total past the limit is billed whole; fine-tuned models, fine-tuning, evals and tool use are not included. The search fee is billed for certain; whether a turn that searched also loses the allowance for its own tokens is to confirm when web search is first switched on (phase 3), and until then the plan treats such a turn as billed.
- Osmo's share: 700,000 a day from the mini pool and 70,000 from the large pool (`decisions.md`, 2026-09-29 evening; it was 500,000 and 50,000 earlier that day); the investing agents are capped at the rest, with `OPENAI_RESERVE_FRACTION=0.28` in their project so the two systems together stay inside 2.5 million and 250,000. Osmo cannot see their usage, so it keeps strictly to its share.
- The counter resets at 00:00 UTC (the help article, quoted by several sources, and confirmed by an independent implementation). The ledger is keyed by the UTC date in one function, `dayKey`, so a different hour would be a one-line change.
- Sharing is switched on per project in the OpenAI dashboard, and only shared projects get the free tokens. The account also needs a positive balance for them to apply (to confirm).
- The four rules, each with a test: **listed models only** (one `ALLOWED_MODELS` list, dated, copied from the dashboard; a model constant not in it fails the test suite); **count every token** (input and output, the output including reasoning, from every call, tool rounds included); **stop short of each cap** (a call is refused when today's count plus the call's estimate would cross the pool's cap, which is OpenAI's own per-request rule applied by Osmo before the request instead of by OpenAI after it); **fall back to the rule-based chain** when refused or on any error.

### Which models
- **Conversation: `gpt-5.4-mini`**, from the mini pool. Released 2026-03-17, the newest listed mini model; 400,000-token context (272,000 in), knowledge to August 2025; it runs with reasoning off by default, so the share is not spent on thinking, and at that setting it answers in about one to three seconds. At about 4,500 tokens a message (a 4,000-token prompt plus a short reply) the 500,000 share is roughly 110 messages a day; a 50,000 share of `gpt-5.4` would be about 11, which is why the large model is not the conversation model.
- **Summaries and weekly patterns (phases 3 and 4): `gpt-5.4`**, from the large pool. A few calls a day, no history window, so 50,000 tokens is plenty.
- **Alternatives, all listed:** `gpt-4.1-mini` is the fallback choice if `gpt-5.4-mini` proves off-tone; it never reasons, has no shutdown date, and its knowledge stops in mid-2024. `gpt-5-mini` is not a good swap: it cannot turn reasoning off (its lowest setting is `minimal`, which still spends tokens) and its dated snapshot is shut down on 2026-12-11. `gpt-5.4-nano` is not worth its savings, because the prompt is the cost, not the reply. Six names on the dashboard's lists are being retired: `o4-mini` and `gpt-4.1-nano` shut down on 2026-10-23, and `o3`, `gpt-5`, `gpt-5-mini` and `gpt-5-nano` (each has only its 2025 dated snapshot) on 2026-12-11. The model is one constant per pool in `allowance.ts`, and the eval set in phase 4 is how a change is judged.
- The SDK's `ChatModel` union lists every model on the dashboard's lists, so the names are ones this SDK version knows; but the request's `model` is typed `ResponsesModel`, which is `(string & {}) | ChatModel | ...`, so any string type-checks, and the type is no guard for the list: the `ALLOWED_MODELS` test is. The dated snapshot for the mini model is `gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17`. Use the bare names, so OpenAI's own upgrades apply.
- A note from the field: in March 2026 several people reported `gpt-5.4-mini` traffic counted against the large pool instead of the mini pool, and free tokens have been mis-billed more than once. The first-day check below is how Osmo finds out which pool he actually landed in.

### Which API, and the request
- **The Responses API** (`client.responses.create`), which the SDK's own README calls the primary API. It has the `instructions` field for the system prompt, the built-in `web_search` tool (Chat Completions has no such tool, only a `web_search_options` field), `max_output_tokens`, and a usage block that breaks out reasoning and cached tokens. Nothing in the route uses Chat Completions.
- The `openai` package needs Node 22 or later; the Vercel project runs Node 24. The package is not in `package.json` yet; language adds it.
- The request, per turn:
  - `model`: the conversation constant.
  - `instructions`: the system prompt from `prompt.ts`.
  - `input`: the history window and the new message as `{ role, content }` items. Within one turn's tool loop, the model's output items are replayed in order and the tool results appended as `function_call_output` items with the `call_id` the model gave; the SDK's `toResponseInputItems()` does the normalising. Across turns nothing is replayed: the next turn is rebuilt from `messages` as plain text.
  - `reasoning: { effort: "none" }` for conversation. On the 5.4 family the accepted values are `none`, `low`, `medium`, `high` and `xhigh`; `none` is the default and there is no `minimal` (the API rejects it by name). Sent explicitly anyway, so a later model change cannot silently start spending on thought. The summaries use `low`. Do not vary it between requests: a change invalidates the prompt cache.
  - `text: { verbosity: "low" }`: his replies are short. Also fixed, for the same reason.
  - `max_output_tokens`: 400 for conversation; the cap covers reasoning tokens too, and at `none` there are none. If a response comes back `incomplete` with reason `max_output_tokens`, the text so far is used when it ends a sentence, else the fallback answers. "Ends a sentence" means the trimmed `output_text` matches `/[.!?]['")\]]?$/`. Either way the call is counted as in Counting below: from its `usage` when the response carries one, else the estimate.
  - `store: false`: nothing is kept on OpenAI's side for retrieval (the default keeps responses for at least 30 days), and no `previous_response_id`; the route replays history itself. With `store` off, `include: ["reasoning.encrypted_content"]` is set so any reasoning item can be replayed inside the tool loop. Whether `store: false` interacts with the sharing programme is not documented anywhere the cloud session could find; the first-day check settles it, and if the free tokens did not apply, `store` goes back to its default.
  - `tools`: the function tools with `strict: true` and `parallel_tool_calls: false`, so each round is one call and the loop stays simple; `tool_choice: "auto"`. Strict mode means every schema is an object with `additionalProperties: false` and every property listed in `required`; an optional argument is expressed as a nullable required one (`"days": { "type": ["integer", "null"] }`), and the model passes `null` when it has no value. The schemas in `lib/agent/tools/*.ts` are plain JSON Schema objects, and one test in that folder asserts these rules for every tool schema, because the API rejects a non-conforming strict schema with a 400 on every request that offers tools, which the fake-client tests would never see. Web search is `{ type: "web_search", search_context_size: "low" }`, only when the switch is on and the day's search count allows; the SDK's create parameters have no per-request cap on built-in tool calls, so the count is kept in the ledger from the `web_search_call` output items. OpenAI's web-search guide says `gpt-5.4` with reasoning effort `none` may produce lower-quality search results (the mini is not named); a searched turn may therefore want `low`, at the price of a prompt-cache miss on that turn and reasoning tokens inside `max_output_tokens`. Decide on the first day with the switch on.
  - `safety_identifier`: a hash of Gur's user id, as OpenAI asks of apps with end users; the old `user` field is deprecated.
  - `prompt_cache_key`: the same hash, never the raw user id. It steers requests with the same prefix to the same cache. No `temperature` or `top_p`: the family accepts them only with reasoning off, and the default is right for a companion.
- Streaming is not used in phase 1 (see the phase). If it is added later, `client.responses.stream()` is the helper: the text arrives as `response.output_text.delta` events, and the finished `Response`, with `usage`, arrives in `response.completed` (or `response.incomplete` or `response.failed`); the helper's `finalResponse()` returns that last object. The ledger is written from it, never from the deltas.
- Prompt caching is automatic and prefix-based, which is why the stable blocks come first; the SDK reports `usage.input_tokens_details.cached_tokens`. On models before the 5.6 family (newer than anything on the dashboard's lists, so not usable here) the minimum cacheable prefix "varies by request settings", and the mini models are not on the list for 24-hour retention, so expect hits within a conversation and not across hours. The feelings block is inside `instructions`, so hits cover the prefix up to it and not the conversation items; caching the history too would mean moving the per-turn feelings into a developer item just before the newest message, a separate decision. Cached input is cheaper on the bill; this plan still counts cached tokens in full against the share, because nothing says the allowance discounts them.

### Counting
- After every call, add `usage.input_tokens + usage.output_tokens` (which is `usage.total_tokens`) to the ledger. `output_tokens` includes the reasoning tokens (`usage.output_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens` is the breakdown, recorded too); `input_tokens` includes the cached ones (`usage.input_tokens_details.cached_tokens`, recorded too). Store the model, so the pool is known.
- Before every call, estimate: the instructions and input in characters divided by three, rounded up, plus `max_output_tokens`. If `used(pool, today) + estimate > cap(pool)`, the call is not made and the turn falls back. The estimate is deliberately generous, so the counter always stops short. The SDK also has an exact counter, `client.responses.inputTokens.count(...)`; it is a network call, so it is for calibrating the estimator in tests, not for every turn.
- A call that times out, fails with a connection error, or gets a 5xx after the request was sent may still have been served: count the estimate for it (`APIConnectionTimeoutError`, `APIConnectionError`, `InternalServerError`; the connection errors carry `status: undefined`). A 4xx answer (`AuthenticationError` 401, `RateLimitError` 429 including `insufficient_quota`, `BadRequestError` 400, any other `APIError` with `status < 500`) says nothing was served: count nothing. A call the ledger refuses before sending counts nothing.
- A tool loop is several calls; each one is estimated, checked and counted on its own, so a long loop can stop in the middle and fall back.
- The two caps are the server settings `OSMO_MINI_TOKENS_PER_DAY` and `OSMO_LARGE_TOKENS_PER_DAY`, required, with no defaults in code: an absent cap means that pool is off. The first values are 630000 and 63000, a tenth under the share (Gur's 10% margin, `decisions.md`), until the dashboard has shown a few real days: OpenAI counts on its own clock, the investing agents' estimates may be off in the other direction, and the people who have built this before stop at ninety percent.
- When the mini pool is past 80% of its cap for the day, the history window drops from 30 messages to 10, so the last fifth goes further.
- Two tabs could race by one call, because `sendText` returns false only within one browser; the margin covers that.

### Errors, timeouts and the fallback
- The conversation client is built with `timeout: 15_000` and `maxRetries: 0`: one attempt per request, then the code answers. The SDK's defaults are a 10-minute timeout and two retries with backoff, on connection errors, 408, 409, 429 and 5xx; for a chat turn a retry is a longer wait and possibly a second spend, and the fallback is free. The tool loop's 40-second turn deadline (phase 2) shortens later rounds' `timeout` through the per-request options.
- The summary client (phases 3 and 4) is built with `timeout: 60_000` and `maxRetries: 1`: no browser waits for it, so one retry is affordable, but it runs in `after()` inside the same invocation, so its worst case, two attempts and about two minutes with backoff, fits the 300-second limit below. The SDK's default ten-minute timeout is never used; no Vercel function lives that long.
- Catch the SDK's classes from most specific to least: `AuthenticationError` (401, the key), `RateLimitError` (429; a spent balance also arrives here, with `error.code === "insufficient_quota"`, so log the code), `APIConnectionTimeoutError`, `APIConnectionError`, `InternalServerError`, then `APIError`. All of them fall back to `processTurn`. The response tells the browser `source: "fallback"` and why, so a bad key is visible in the console rather than mistaken for a dull mood. Every fallback path hands `processTurn` the pre-`prepareTurn` state and session, as phase 1 says.
- A missing key is not an error path: the route sees no `OPENAI_API_KEY` and no `CHATGPT_KEY` and falls back before building a client (the SDK throws at construction without a key). The same for a missing `OSMO_OWNER_ID` (`reason: "no_owner"`) and a missing mini cap (`reason: "off"`).
- A refusal by the model (`incomplete_details.reason === "content_filter"`, or a `refusal` content part) falls back the same way. Unlikely for a companion.
- Vercel: the project was created after Fluid compute became the default, where a function may run 300 seconds on every plan, so a 40-second turn plus its Supabase work needs no `maxDuration` export. If a build ever says otherwise, `export const maxDuration = 60` in the route is the fix.

### Cost
- Inside the allowance: nothing. A day of conversation is bounded by the share, and the fallback takes over after it. For scale, if the counting slipped by a whole day's share on `gpt-5.4-mini` (about $0.75 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output, to confirm) the bill would be under a dollar.
- Outside it: web search, when switched on, is $10 per thousand calls plus the search content tokens at the model's rate, and the turn's own tokens are treated as billed (to confirm); the speaking voice's text-to-speech (`gpt-4o-mini-tts`, about $12 per million audio tokens, roughly a cent and a half a minute of speech) is billed once Gur switches `naturalVoice` on and the key is on Vercel (it is off and unpushed today). A monthly spend limit in the OpenAI dashboard is the backstop for both, and for any slip in the counting.

The ledger, for main to apply, written like the live tables (own rows only, `user_id` defaulting to `auth.uid()`, the policy for `authenticated`), with the function's `search_path` pinned so the Supabase advisor stays at its one known warning:

```sql
create table public.token_ledger (
  user_id uuid not null default auth.uid() references auth.users (id) on delete cascade,
  day date not null,
  pool text not null check (pool in ('mini', 'large')),
  model text not null,
  calls integer not null default 0 check (calls >= 0),
  input_tokens integer not null default 0 check (input_tokens >= 0),
  output_tokens integer not null default 0 check (output_tokens >= 0),
  reasoning_tokens integer not null default 0 check (reasoning_tokens >= 0),
  cached_tokens integer not null default 0 check (cached_tokens >= 0),
  searches integer not null default 0 check (searches >= 0),
  updated_at timestamptz not null default now(),
  primary key (user_id, day, pool, model)
);
alter table public.token_ledger enable row level security;
create policy "own token_ledger" on public.token_ledger for all to authenticated
  using ((select auth.uid()) = user_id) with check ((select auth.uid()) = user_id);

create or replace function public.add_tokens(
  p_day date, p_pool text, p_model text,
  p_input integer, p_output integer, p_reasoning integer, p_cached integer,
  p_searches integer default 0
) returns void language sql security invoker set search_path = '' as $$
  insert into public.token_ledger as t
    (user_id, day, pool, model, calls, input_tokens, output_tokens, reasoning_tokens, cached_tokens, searches)
  values ((select auth.uid()), p_day, p_pool, p_model, 1, p_input, p_output, p_reasoning, p_cached, p_searches)
  on conflict (user_id, day, pool, model) do update set
    calls = t.calls + 1,
    input_tokens = t.input_tokens + excluded.input_tokens,
    output_tokens = t.output_tokens + excluded.output_tokens,
    reasoning_tokens = t.reasoning_tokens + excluded.reasoning_tokens,
    cached_tokens = t.cached_tokens + excluded.cached_tokens,
    searches = t.searches + excluded.searches,
    updated_at = now();
$$;
```

The function runs as the caller, so row-level security still applies, and the increment is one statement, so two calls cannot lose each other's count. Today's use for a pool is the sum of `input_tokens + output_tokens` over the day's rows for that pool, and today's searches the sum of `searches`; the route selects those rows (one per model, so a handful at most) and sums in code, because aggregate functions are off by default on Supabase's API. The route never uses a service-role key.

## Code structure
All new files are the language lane's, which owns the conversation and can test with the key locally.
- `app/api/chat/route.ts`: auth and the owner check, load, `prepareTurn`, today's use (for `historyWindow` and the ledger), the prompt, `ask`, save, respond. Thin; everything it calls is testable without it.
- `app/api/chat/openai.ts`: the client, and one `ask(request, ledger)` that runs the tool loop. Before every round, the first included, it estimates that round's request with `estimateTokens` and asks `ledger.mayCall(model, estimate)`; when told no it stops and returns `{ refused: true, calls }`, and the route falls back with `reason: "allowance"`. After every round it calls `ledger.record({ model, usage, status, searches })` before running any tool; if the round threw or timed out it records the round's estimate instead (when the rule in Counting says so) and then rethrows the SDK's error, so every round that was sent is counted whatever happens next. Returns `{ text, calls }` on success. The only file that imports `openai`.
- `app/api/chat/ledger.ts`: `makeLedger(client, caps, day)` returns the `ledger` that `ask` receives: `usedToday(pool)` (the day's rows for the pool, summed in code), `mayCall(model, estimate)` on top of `allowance.ts`'s pure `mayCall(used, estimate, cap)`, and `record(...)`, one `add_tokens` call per model call; a call that timed out after sending is recorded as `p_input = estimate - max_output_tokens, p_output = max_output_tokens`, matching how the estimate is built; a refused call is never recorded. The only place the route reads or writes `token_ledger` (the phase 4 panels read it from the browser under the same RLS). Tested with a fake client.
- `app/api/chat/supabase.ts`: the per-request client, built with the bearer token after `requireUser` in speaking's `lib/server/auth.ts` (unpushed as of 2026-09-29; null means 401) has verified it.
- `lib/agent/prompt.ts`: state in, system prompt out. Pure.

### The `/api/chat` contract
`POST` with `Authorization: Bearer <Supabase access token>` (checked by `requireUser` from speaking's `lib/server/auth.ts`, unpushed as of 2026-09-29) and body `{ text: string, speaker: "you" | "guest", session: Session, day: string, greet?: boolean }`. `session` is the browser's `Session` from `mind.ts`, returned updated; `day` is the browser's calendar day; `greet` is the voice's first-line flag from `SendOptions` (only the voice knows it, so the route never infers it) and is ignored unless `speaker` is `"guest"`. Response `{ reply: string, state: AgentState, session: Session, source: "model" | "fallback", reason?: "off" | "allowance" | "error" | "no_key" | "no_owner" | "crisis", usage: { pool: "mini" | "large", usedToday: number, cap: number } }`, or a 401 (no valid token) or 403 (a valid token that is not the owner's). The route saves the two `messages` rows and `agent_state` itself, so `sendText` only shows the reply and takes the new state for the mood theme. For a guest turn `state` is the state as loaded, unchanged: the prepared state is used for the prompt and then discarded, so the browser's theme does not move; the browser applies `state` on every 200 without checking `speaker`. `usage` is on every 200, fallback and guest turns included, so the route reads the ledger before any short-circuit; `pool` is the conversation model's pool, `mini` in every phase (the large pool's summaries are a separate job, and per-pool figures are the Insights panel's own query in phase 4); `usedToday` is today's use for that pool including this turn's calls (the value the ledger check read plus what this turn counted), and `cap` is that pool's cap from its server setting. `reason` on a `source: "model"` reply is absent; `"crisis"` marks the code's crisis reply. `session`, `day`, `greet`, `reason` and `usage` are additions to the draft in `project.md`. Streaming, if added, ends in the same final object.

## Risks and open assumptions
- **Going over the allowance.** OpenAI bills past the limit instead of stopping. The ledger, the generous estimate, the caps set under the share, and the monthly spend limit are four separate guards; the investing agents' own cap is the fifth, and it is not Osmo's to check. The first days are watched on the dashboard.
- **Someone else spending the key.** Sign-ups are still open on the Supabase project, so a stranger could hold a valid token. The owner check is the guard until Gur closes them, and stays after.
- **The sharing trade.** Everything Osmo sends, in a prompt or to the natural voice, may be used by OpenAI to improve its models. That is Gur's decision to confirm before phase 1 goes live, and his self-description must say plainly that his words leave the device. The caps are the switch, so nothing goes live on a push until he sets them.
- **Free tokens landing in the wrong pool, or not at all.** It has happened to others with this model family. The first-day check catches it, and until then the caps sit a tenth under the share.
- **The model list moves.** The dashboard's list changes with each model generation, and two names on it retire next month. `ALLOWED_MODELS` is one dated list; when it no longer matches the dashboard, the test that pins it is the reminder.
- **The reset hour** is assumed to be 00:00 UTC. If the dashboard shows otherwise, `dayKey` changes.
- **Latency.** A model reply takes one to three seconds where today's is instant (about 0.8 seconds to the first token and 160 tokens a second for this model with reasoning off, per public measurements). The typewriter and the "One moment…" line cover waiting; the per-request timeout of 15 seconds and the 40-second turn deadline bound the worst case.
- **Character drift.** A model will happily be warmer or chattier than Osmo should be. The prompt rules and the eval set are the guard. Keeping the heart and bond as code, not prose, is the other: the model is told he is a stranger, it does not get to decide.
- **Invented memories.** The strongest rule in the prompt: never claim to remember something that is not in the memory block or a tool result. The eval checks it.
- **Privacy.** Messages now leave the browser to Vercel and to OpenAI, and from phase 2 the word definitions are fetched from the server. That is a change from "internet only for word definitions" and his self-description must say so plainly. Supabase rows stay as private as before.
- **Guest leakage.** The model is the one place a guest could be told something of Gur's, because it answers freely. The guest request, prompt, history and tools alike, must contain nothing of his, and the route test for a guest turn asserts that no memory sentence, name, history line, bond stage or cause reaches the model and that `define` is the only tool offered. The voice check itself (who is speaking) stays on the device and is untouched.
